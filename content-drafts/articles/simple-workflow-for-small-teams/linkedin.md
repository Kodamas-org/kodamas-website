"We're only three people, we don't need a process."

I have heard this in every small company I worked for and with. And every time, those three people had produced an impressive amount of documents, passwords and half-made decisions. The login for the analytics tool was saved in someone's browser. Nobody knew whose.

Small teams don't need a PM. They need a small set of good practices:

• recurring meetings with a simple agenda
• decisions written down, so they don't get decided again (differently)
• everything in a place everyone knows
• goals small enough to actually finish
• accesses never in one person's hands

The columns on your board matter much less than you think. Sorry to everyone hoping for a template.

The part people skip is the most important one: a process is an agreement, so it needs to be checked and renegotiated. At Kodamas, Emma and I used to meet three times a week. We kept cancelling one, so now we have two. Nobody broke a rule, the rule was just wrong for the moment.

The signals are always small: meetings that get cancelled, "how do I access this?", bugs reported in DMs, a founder who doesn't know who is working on what.

When I see them, I don't call a meeting about "our communication problems". I start using the tool myself, then bring the closest people along, and only then I bring it up with everyone.

If anything, being small is the easiest moment to start a process.
