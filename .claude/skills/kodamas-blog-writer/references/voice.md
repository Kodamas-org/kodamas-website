# Daniela's voice

The voice always wins over SEO. If a rule here clashes with an SEO rule, the voice rule wins.

## Tone

- **Slightly fun and professional at the same time.** Something that makes you laugh and also makes you think. Humour comes from honest, specific observations (the things everyone in a small team recognises), not from jokes added on top.
- **Never judgmental, never self-pitying, never bitter.** Not about past jobs, former employers, founders, colleagues, clients or bad situations. Describe what happened and what was learned, with warmth. Messy situations are normal and human, nobody is the villain.
- **Confident without lecturing.** Share what worked and what didn't from real experience. Opinions are welcome; preaching is not.
- **English only.**

## Person: "I" and "we"

Mix "I" (Daniela, her own experience and opinions) and "we" (Kodamas, what Daniela and Emma do and believe), even in the same article. Use "I" for stories from her past roles, "we" for how Kodamas works now. "You" addresses the reader directly.

## Grammar and style

- **Absolutely no em dashes (—). Not one.** Also avoid en dashes (–) used as punctuation. Use commas, full stops, colons or brackets instead. Hyphens inside compound words (post-launch, two-person) are fine.
- **Italian-ish structures are part of the voice.** Don't "correct" them into perfect native English. Only fix real errors that change the meaning or make a sentence unclear, and if in doubt, ask.
- Short paragraphs. Varied sentence length. Plain words over clever ones.
- Concrete over abstract: real tools, real roles, real numbers, real moments.

## Never sound AI-made

Banned phrases and words (and their close variants):

- "In today's fast-paced world", "in today's digital landscape", "in an ever-evolving…"
- "Let's dive in", "dive deep", "deep dive", "let's unpack", "let's explore"
- "game-changer", "unlock", "unleash", "delve", "harness", "leverage" (as a verb), "elevate", "empower" as filler, "supercharge", "streamline" as filler
- "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge", "holistic", "synergy", "pivotal", "crucial", "vital", "myriad", "plethora", "tapestry", "realm", "landscape", "navigate the complexities", "journey" (for non-journeys)
- "Here's the thing", "The truth is", "The reality is", "Spoiler alert", "Buckle up", "Picture this", "Imagine a world where"
- "It's worth noting that", "It's important to remember", "At the end of the day", "When it comes to"
- "In conclusion", "To sum up", "All in all", "Ultimately," as an opener
- "Whether you're a X or a Y…" openers

Banned patterns:

- **"It's not X, it's Y"** and "Not just X, but Y" contrasts. Use at most rarely, and never as a rhetorical device.
- **Triplets** of adjectives or parallel phrases ("clear, simple and effective") as a habit.
- **Rhetorical questions** in every paragraph. One now and then is fine.
- **Over-polished symmetry**: every section the same length, every list with the same number of items, every paragraph ending with a punchline.
- **A closing summary** that repeats everything already said. End on a real thought, a story, or simply stop.
- A **bolded lead-in on every bullet** ("**Clarity:** …"), emoji in headings, or lists where prose would read better.
- **Colon reveals** in every other sentence ("The answer: documentation.").
- One-line dramatic paragraphs used as a habit ("And that changed everything.").
- Hedging stacks ("can potentially help to possibly…") and empty intensifiers ("truly", "incredibly", "absolutely essential").
- Generic examples ("a company might…") where a real one from Daniela exists.
- Signposting the structure ("In this article we will…", "Now that we've covered X, let's move to Y").
